JitJet: Journey is the Jet. ONE bbbar event and the SAME event followed at every STAGE.


This is the constitution of the JitJet book, which every chapter has to follow. Since we focus only on the ONE bbbar event, we can keep generatig only ONE bbbar event (in case of statistical limitation, we can generate more for illustration)

Rule A: Every chapter XYZ has 
    (A1) test/XYZ/prefix_XYZ.sh/.py            the scripts
    (A2) test/XYZ/out/prefix_XYZ.log/.root/.pdf       logs, root, plots (just the pdf from this chapter)
    (A3) doc/chapter/chapter_XYZ.tex the book chapter: physics
    (A4) doc/chapter/chapter_XYZ.md  the technical companion, not compiled into the .tex

Rule B: The doc/chapter/chapter_XYZ.tex has exactly these sections (B1-B5, and (B6) only when it applies)
    (B1) What this chapter is about in 1-2 paragraphs 
    (B2) connection to the previous chapter in 1-2 paragraphs
    (B3) the numbers supporting/explaining the physics of the chapter.
    (B4) ledger table summerising  how much energy was added/removed from the event and the eta-phi display plot
    (B5) open problems for the Phase-2 LHC, after a DETAILED digging inside CMSSW (e.g. what cuts/thresholds/calibrations have not been updated since Run-2 or Run-3 
    (B6) OPTIONAL hand-over section "Made here for the next stage: ...", placed between (B3) and (B4), in 2-3 pages: when a stage makes
         something that is not used by the stage itself but by a LATER stage, it is explained where it is MADE: what it is,
         how it is made, why it is made here and not in the stage that uses it, and its numbers for our event.
         At most one per chapter. The first case: the calorimeter "TriggerPrimitiveDigis" (simEcalTriggerPrimitiveDigis,
         simHcalTriggerPrimitiveDigis) are made in 11_DIGI and used in 12_L1T, so they are explained in chapter_11_DIGI.tex;
         chapter_12_L1T.tex only recalls them and refers back.

Rule C: The doc/chapter/chapter_XYZ.md companion has exactly these sections
    (C1) The cmsDriver commands. How to run and what each argument mean
    (C2) The cmsRun on python config commands. How to run and a quick walk-through for different parts of the python config. What is the content of the output root file.
    (C3) The cmsRun analyser config commands. A quick overview and what it producess (pdf, log) 
    (C4) The full/partial tables: every number the doc/chapter/chapter_XYZ.tex quotes

Rule D:
 
    All chapters/*.tex are included in doc/JitJetSW.tex file which is compiled and produces the final pdf

Rule E: 
    The output of previous chapter goes as input to the current chapter and so on

Rule F: 
    The test dir can hold common scripts to avoid duplications with test/XYZ

Rule G: Checkpoint chapters (12_L1T, 13_HLT)
    A checkpoint is a stage which does not add/remove energy from the event: it looks at the event and DECIDES whether the event is kept.
    The decision is taken in several major steps, so a checkpoint chapter is big and is broken into sections, one per step:
    (G1) doc/chapter/chapter_XYZ.tex has (B1), (B2), then ONE SECTION PER MAJOR STEP in place of (B3), then (B4) and (B5)
    (G2) every step section is 2-3 pages and illustrates, for our event, step by step how the decision is built: what the step receives
         from the previous step, what it does (the algorithm in plain words), its parameters/thresholds, the numbers of our event,
         and an eta-phi display plot showing how the event looks AFTER this step
    (G3) the ledger of (B4) has two parts: the energy the trigger SEES from step to step (nothing is added to or removed from the recorded
         event), and the DECISION: which algorithms/paths fired and by which margin; the display is the event as the last step sees it
    (G4) the plugins analyse the different steps (one plugin per group of steps, one pdf per plugin); ONE sample only, the mixed event
         (Rule H). What the pileup does to the decision is read from the truth the file still carries (the genJets of the signal,
         the simulated particles behind an object), not from separate signal and pileup samples
    (G5) the companion (C1)-(C4) is unchanged; (C4) lists the numbers step by step
    The steps:
        12_L1T: (1) calorimeter trigger primitives (ECAL, HCAL): made in the DIGI job, explained in chapter 11 (B6), recalled here
                (2) calorimeter Layer-1: receives, calibrates and sums the trigger primitives into trigger towers
                (3) calorimeter Layer-2: jets, e/gamma, taus, energy sums
                (4) muon trigger primitives and the muon track finders (BMTF, OMTF, EMTF)
                (5) the global muon trigger (uGMT)
                (6) the global trigger (uGT): every algorithm of the menu in parallel, the final decision
        13_HLT: (1) the raw event (DIGI2RAW), the L1 seed and the prescale: hltGtStage2Digis, HLTL1TSeed, HLTPrescaler
                (2) calorimeter jets: ECAL/HCAL rec-hits, calorimeter towers, AK4 calo jets and their corrections, the calo-jet cut
                (3) tracking and vertices: pixel tracks and vertices, the vertices kept, the iterative tracking, the PF vertices
                (4) particle flow and PF jets: PF candidates, AK4 PF jets, rho and the jet energy corrections
                (5) the decision of the paths: the last filters, TriggerResults, the datasets and the streams
                The menu is the GRun menu of the release (the 2025 menu; HLT:@relval2024 is a fake menu); the seeds that name
                algorithms missing from the 2024 L1 menu of 12_L1T are adapted by python/hltL1Menu_cff.py (Rule E: it reads
                the L1 menu from the output of 12_L1T). The path followed through the steps is HLT_PFJet110, seeded by
                L1_SingleJet90.

Rule H: Signal and pileup are followed separately only up to 11_DIGI
    The DIGI stage is the LAST stage at which the signal and the pileup exist apart. Up to 11_DIGI (08_PU, 09_G4, 10_SIM, 11_DIGI)
    a chapter may show the three samples: signal alone, one pileup collision alone, signal + pileup (mixed).
    (H1) from 12_L1T onwards (12_L1T, 13_HLT and every later stage) ONLY the mixed event is followed: no separate signal or pileup
         sample, in the scripts (one cmsDriver/cmsRun, one analyzer job, no <case> in the file names), in the .tex and in the .md
    (H2) the input (Rule E) of 12_L1T is therefore the mixed file of 11_DIGI only: test/11_DIGI/out/step3_DIGI_mixed_11_DIGI.root
    (H3) anything that still needs the signal alone or the pileup alone (e.g. the trigger primitives of the signal alone) is
         computed and shown in 11_DIGI, the last chapter where it exists; later chapters quote it from there

Rule I: The CMS chapters use the settings of the release year, 2025
    CMSSW_15_0_5 is a release of 2025, so the CMS-centric chapters (08_PU, 09_G4, 10_SIM, 11_DIGI, 12_L1T, 13_HLT) run with the
    2025 Run-3 settings of the release (upgradeWorkflowComponents.py, key '2025'): --conditions auto:phase1_2025_realistic
    (150X_mcRun3_2025_realistic_v1), --era Run3_2025, --geometry DB:Extended, --beamspot DBrealistic, 13.6 TeV, and in
    13_HLT the HLT menu of that key, -s HLT:@relval2025 (= 2025v10).
    The year of a chapter is the variable YEAR of test/common/jitjet_common.sh (CONDITIONS and ERA follow from it).
    (I1) the generator chapters 01_ME .. 07_GenJet keep the 2024 settings (auto:phase1_2024_realistic, Run3_2024): at generator
         level the conditions only give the beam spot of the vertex smearing (SimBeamSpotObjectsRcd), the same payload in both
         global tags
    (I2) only the CMS settings (global tag, era, the defaults of the analyzer configs, the labels) move to 2025: the event, the
         pileup (PU = 60, bunch crossings -1..1, SKIP = 71, NPU = 68), the number of events and the seeds stay the same
    (I3) a chapter moved to 2025 is rerun and compared with its 2024 output; what the 2025 conditions change in the event (or the
         fact that nothing changes, and why) is said in its .tex (B2 or B3) and listed with the evidence (conddb, the compared
         files) in its .md (C4)
    (I4) the move is made in two steps:
         (a) 08_PU, 09_G4, 10_SIM: done. The 2025 global tag has the geometry (141DD4hepV2_Extended2024), the field map and the
             simulated beam spot of 2024, and the five modifiers Run3_2025 adds to Run3_2024 act only in RECO and L1: the outputs
             are identical to the 2024 ones, collision by collision and hit by hit
         (b) 11_DIGI, 12_L1T, 13_HLT: done. They read conditions that do differ (ECAL selective-readout thresholds, laser
             transparency and trigger-primitive tables, HCAL SiPM parameters, the pixel Lorentz angle and templates, the L1
             menu L1Menu_Collisions2025_v1_0_0, the CICADA model of the era), and their numbers changed: fewer ECAL barrel
             digis, no Level-1 jets in tower ring 28 (and with them no VBF algorithm fires), 63 of 465 L1 algorithms;
             the HLT menu is the one the release gives the 2025 RelVals, HLT:@relval2025 = 2025v10 (module for module
             GRun), complete (898 paths), since its seeds match the 2025 L1 menu. The path of the book, L1_SingleJet90 ->
             HLT_PFJet110, accepts the event with either year; each chapter has a "What the year changed" paragraph
